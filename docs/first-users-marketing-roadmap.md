## 3a. Why they'd switch: the competitive wedge

Almost no independent handyman who already quotes and invoices is using *nothing*. They are using pen and paper, a notes app, Square Invoices, or a free trade-specific tool such as Joist. So the first real objection in most qualified conversations will be some form of **“why pay $19.99/month when what I have works — and some of it is free?”** You need an answer ready before the first call, and you need to find out fast whether the answer is even true for that person.

**The likely wedge (state it plainly, don't oversell it):** TradeReady's reusable pricebook turns a quote into an invoice without re-entering the line items, and keeps one customer/job record across both documents. The value is *time and consistency on repeat work* — the same drywall patch or paint prep priced the same way every time, with no double entry. That is the specific thing pen-and-paper and generic invoice apps make you redo by hand.

**Use it as a diagnostic, not a pitch.** After “what do you use now,” ask “what does that *not* do that you wish it did?” Then listen for whether re-entry, inconsistent pricing, or quote→invoice rework is a real pain for them. If it is, the wedge is live and you lead with it. If, across five qualified conversations, no recurring gap surfaces that the free tools don't already cover, **that is a more important finding than any activation number** — it means the segment or the value proposition is wrong, and you should treat it as a kill/pivot signal (see §6 and §9), not push harder on the same message.

**One honest price line for objections (no discount):** “It's $19.99 a month after the trial. The reason it can be worth that over a free invoice app is the reusable pricebook — you price a job once and reuse it on every similar quote and its invoice, so you're not re-typing line items. If you don't do enough repeat-type work for that to matter, it probably isn't worth it for you, and that's a fair answer.” Saying the last part builds trust and disqualifies bad fits faster.

## 6. Four bounded experiments

### Experiment 1 — One upcoming job, one workflow

**Audience/objective:** Personally qualify the first twelve handymen and recruit users around an immediate estimate or invoice.

**Mechanism/message:** The upcoming job creates a reason to try the app now. Offer: “Bring one estimate you need to send; I will help you set it up in a 20-minute session. You choose whether the app is worth continuing.” This is an improvement to the existing concierge idea.

**Execution:** Ask about current workflow first, including what their current tool doesn't do that they wish it did (§3a). Demonstrate using fictional data, then let the owner enter their own information on their own device. They review scope, amounts, recipient, and delivery. Set up no more than three reusable services. No historical migration or payment-account setup unless needed and specifically requested.

**Budget/cap:** $0 new spend; first twelve approaches plus follow-up, about three hours of research/contact work; at most 30 minutes of setup/support per participant in week one. Expect learning and possible first use, not a guaranteed conversion rate.

**Measure/decision:** After twelve delivered first approaches and one follow-up, fewer than two substantive responses triggers a review of targeting and opening message. After five qualified conversations, no recurring problem that the current app solves — including no gap beyond what free tools already cover (§3a) — triggers a segment/use-case or value-proposition change. If two of the first three willing testers cannot finish the core task, pause recruitment to fix the obstacle. Expand in small batches only when someone completes the workflow and agrees to try a second job.

### Experiment 2 — Website handoff into an estimate

**Audience/objective:** Groundwork first, PhoenixWebDesigner second; obtain one permission-based introduction to a contractor launching or updating a website.

**Mechanism/message:** The designer can offer a practical next step after a quote inquiry arrives; TradeReady gains an introduction at a workflow-selection moment. The product does not generate traffic or guarantee customer leads.

**Execution:** After verifying the shipped booking link, show one fictional request flowing into the app and becoming an estimate. Ask the partner to forward a short invitation to one suitable client. An interested client decides whether to subscribe and add the link. Have the client/designer retain the existing contact route during the test. Use no custom integration.

**Budget/cap:** $0; two partner approaches, one follow-up each, at most two hours preparation and meetings. Limit the first pilot to one client.

**Measure/decision:** Success is one introduced owner using the app for a real estimate, followed by independent second use. A test-form submission is a setup check, not activation. If both partners decline, lack suitable clients, or require payment/custom development, stop this test for the month. **Execution:** Verify the current export, create a fictional month with invoices, payments, and expenses, and send it only after the reviewer agrees. **Budget/cap:** $0; two approaches, one follow-up each; two hours total. No partnership package or engineering commitment.

**Measure/decision:** Pass the first gate if one reviewer names a specific client use case and considers the output usable. Pass the acquisition gate only if an introduced owner activates. Stop if both require substantial integration or cannot identify a solo-client fit. A friendly review alone is not acquisition success.

**Note on timing:** This experiment needs five *eligible* asks, and eligibility requires independent second use — but this month's target is only three repeat users. So by its own math Experiment 4 will most likely report **“not yet evaluable”** in the first window. That is expected. ## 6a. After someone activates: earn the second use

Independent second use is the north star, and nothing else in the plan actively encourages it — the referral ask (Experiment 4) only fires *after* it already happens. Close that gap with one light-touch, non-salesy move:

- **When you leave the setup session,** ask when their *next* similar job is likely to come up, and get permission for a single check-in around then: “Want me to send one short note when your next drywall job comes up, in case you'd rather reuse the estimate you just built than start over?”
- **Send that one check-in** at the agreed time — no more. Tie it to reusing the pricebook they already built, since that is the wedge (§3a): the second estimate should be faster than the first because the line items already exist.
- **If they take the second estimate on their own,** they are now an independent repeat user and become eligible for Experiment 4. If they don't, that is a fit/value signal to record (§9), not a reason for a second nudge.

No rewards, no drip sequence, no automation. One consented, well-timed touch that lowers the friction on the exact action you're trying to observe.

Do not count multiple jobs as multiple businesses. Report both counts and denominators, such as 3 activated out of 7 qualified conversations. Keep assisted and independent outcomes separate. Ask how the owner found the app and record any partner; do not claim referral tracking survives the App Store automatically. Source attribution is not proof that the channel caused the result.

Only evaluate trial conversion among people whose actual trial has ended. Only evaluate fourteen-day repeat use among users with a full observation window. Check late September starters again around October 13; do not label them churned at day 30.

At day 30:

- **Expand carefully:** At least three independent repeat users, two mature paid continuations, and a repeatable source of qualified conversations. Repeat the best motion; do not broaden to every trade.
- **Fix activation:** People express a relevant need but fail to send their first real document. Pause volume and resolve the observed obstacle.
- **Fix fit/value:** People complete a guided demo but do not use it again, or no gap surfaces beyond what free tools already cover (§3a). Interview them before adding features or partners.
- **Fix targeting/message:** Few relevant replies after the first bounded batches. Change one variable and record it; do not declare an entire market invalid from twelve contacts.

At the listed monthly price, ten monthly subscribers represent $199.90 in gross recurring revenue before platform fees, taxes, refunds, and operating costs—not profit. Actual margins and retention are unknown. Concierge work is a temporary learning expense. If a participant needs more than an hour of support, classify that as a product/fit issue and avoid promising indefinite free assistance.

## 10. What to avoid, and the first moves

Do not buy ads, mailing lists, sponsorships, or directory upgrades. Do not evade subreddit rules, harvest private-group contacts, manufacture testimonials, or offer incentives for favorable reviews. Do not build a referral system, integration, or new dashboard before proving someone needs the existing workflow. Do not put promotional branding on users' customer documents without their approval.

Commercial email rules also apply to business-to-business outreach. Use truthful sender information and subjects, identify the commercial purpose, include a valid physical postal address and clear opt-out, and honor opt-outs. The FTC specifies a maximum of ten business days; this plan uses immediate suppression. The address placeholder in the drafts must be replaced before sending. If you lack an appropriate address, do not improvise one. Review channel and jurisdiction requirements before calling or messaging; this is a conservative operating checklist, not a legal opinion. [FTC CAN-SPAM compliance guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)

For setup, the owner controls the device and account. Use fictional data for shared demonstrations. Do not request credentials, card details, banking information, or customer exports. Obtain specific consent before recording, publishing a case study, naming a customer, or sharing their information with a partner. Do not change subscription terms to recruit users.

**First five moves:**

1. Verify the real estimate-to-invoice flow and actual subscription terms.
2. Log prior outreach so this month adds learning rather than duplicating effort.
3. Prepare the ForgeWorks, Corps Handyman, and RLJ introductions with the correct channel safeguards.
4. Reserve two 20-minute setup slots; offer them only after confirming a real near-term job and fit.
5. After the first reliable real use, approach Groundwork with the website-handoff pilot and BluuPrint with the fictional export-review request.

## 11. Evidence and limits

Research date: August 30, 2026. Official source links sit next to each product or prospect claim. Published contact information was observed, not operationally tested. Website descriptions are businesses' own statements; no revenue, employee count, software stack, license status, or partnership interest was independently verified. The earlier local file, “TradeReady: First Users on a Zero Budget,” supplied context about prior recommendations but was only a summary. The referenced full plan was not available at the checked repository path.

**Two headline risks to watch from day one:**

- **iPhone-only funnel loss.** TradeReady is an iOS pilot, and a large share of trades run Android. Every Android-only prospect — however good a fit otherwise — is disqualified this month. This can remove a substantial fraction of an otherwise-qualified list, so device fit is a gating question, not a footnote (see §3, §4). If qualified conversations keep dying on device, the constraint, not the message, is the bottleneck.
- **No proven wedge over free alternatives.** Most target owners already invoice with pen-and-paper or a free app such as Joist. If five qualified conversations surface no recurring gap the free tools don't already fill (§3a), the value proposition or segment is wrong — a more decisive finding than any activation count, and a reason to pivot rather than push volume.

The roadmap favors direct conversations over a partner-led launch because partner approvals may be slow and the product's independent repeat value is not yet evidenced. Confidence is moderate that these tests will generate useful learning, low that any particular user total will result. Reverse that priority if a partner supplies multiple qualified, opted-in owners who activate and continue with less founder time than direct outreach.
